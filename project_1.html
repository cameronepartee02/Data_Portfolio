The market prices the clock. This model prices the matchup.

Most ticket-pricing advice is built around the calendar: prices are expected to climb right after a sellout, then ease off as kickoff gets close. A Gradient Boosting model trained on nine years of Super Bowl resale data tested that advice directly, and found the opposite. How close you are to kickoff doesn't move the price at all. What moves it is which two teams are playing.

Two signals do almost all the work: which quarterback is returning to the game, and how big the point spread is. Together they explain the large majority of what drives resale price. The calendar explains none of it.

Who benefits from this analysis

The model's value proposition can be pitched from either side of the deal: to the seller, or to the buyer.

Pillar · Revenue

For the seller

On a resale marketplace, the platform doesn't set your price. You do, against every other seller listing the same seat. Every one of those sellers is guessing at the same thing: what a buyer will actually pay for this ticket. The sellers who price closest to that number, fastest, are the ones who capture the sale. This model identifies exactly which levers move a buyer's willingness to pay, so that pricing decision can be made on evidence instead of instinct.

Methodology and key decisions

The modeling choices behind the findings above.

01 · the pivot

"NFL doesn't sell to fans."

No retail price to optimize, since tickets are allocated, not sold. That killed the original NFL-pricing frame before the model was even built.

02 · the data

9 games. 21 rows. Ever.

Resale prices from four platforms, LII–LX. A small, honest sample, worked with rather than padded.

03 · the leakage catch

home_team_won had to go.

Every outcome column, cut on principle. A buyer 10 days out can't see the final score, so training on it isn't prediction. It's cheating.

04 · validation

Every game held out once.

The only defensible cross-validation at this sample size, since the model never trains and tests on the same event.

05 · the tuning

Chose humility over cosmetic gains.

Four tuning rounds. The real gain came from shrinking the model's complexity, not adding more of it. A smaller, steadier model beat a bigger, twitchier one.

06 · the ceiling

31% relative error.

Past "decent," into "meaningful miss." An honest read of what nine data points can support, not a number to round up.

What the data shows

The findings below are the ones that should actually move your hiring decisions, starting with the reframe and ending with the supporting detail.

Pillar · Efficiency

Ranked #1 by mentions. Barely there by reach.

Marketing Analytics Stack is mentioned 31 times across the dataset, more than any other category, which on paper reads as the single most in-demand skill area. But it only shows up at three of the six companies. High volume paired with low reach points to concentration, not demand: the category is likely overloaded within the handful of job descriptions that include it, rather than broadly expected across the market the way its mention count alone suggests.

Pillar · Efficiency

A short list doesn't mean a strict one.

ShopMy and Jump Trading Group both keep their skill lists short, but only about half of what's on them is actually Required, the rest is marked Preferred. Amazon keeps its list just as short and skips the hedge entirely: 11 of 13 skills are Required, almost nothing negotiable.

What I measured but didn't chart

Not every cut we ran earned a place in this deck. Here's what else we looked at, and why it didn't change the recommendation.

Salary by seniority
More senior roles paid more, exactly as expected, and it didn't answer either of the two questions this project started with.
Posting location
Every single posting in the set was based in New York. Nothing to chart when a variable doesn't vary.
Travel allocation
Observed, but pulling on this thread was drifting away from the core skills question the project was built to answer.
Industry mix
Roughly three-quarters of the pool skewed toward consulting-adjacent roles. Coming from a technical background, that was the moment it became clear that strategic positioning, not just technical depth, is the actual gap to close for roles like these.
